# Wildcat Washers — brand direction

**Tucson raised. Wildcat clean.** A neighborhood service company with the confidence and consistency of a brand that can grow.

## Visual identity

- Wildcat Blue: `#3CC9EE`. The memorable brand field, headline color, primary call to action and graphic accent.
- Midnight: `#101C2E`. Typography, navigation, service sections and the oversized footer wordmark.
- Paper: `#F6F5EF`. A clean, understated counterpoint for readable content and real photography.
- Preserve the supplied logo’s existing colors and artwork. Use the outline logo on dark backgrounds and the original logo on light backgrounds.
- Treat the supplied mascot as a stamp of crew pride, not a repeated decorative icon.

## Typography

- Display: **Barlow Condensed**, 500–800. Confident uppercase and tight line spacing. Center the hero headline with enough open space to see the crew film.
- Body and controls: **Figtree**, 400–800. Sentence case for explanations. Clear labels and readable form inputs.
- Outline display text is an occasional accent. It never carries essential instructions.

## Voice

Friendly, local, hardworking, accountable. Talk like the crew that will actually show up. Pair a confident headline with useful, specific service information. Examples: “A spotless reputation.” “Dirt has met its match.” “Not happy? Not done.”

Avoid invented review counts, unverifiable guarantees and generic superlatives. Use shared data for factual claims.

## Imagery and motion

- Show the real team, local properties and real before/after results.
- Documentary crew footage with muted color in the hero; full-color photography in service and proof sections.
- “Let the light in” is the interactive brand moment: a full-viewport, fogged window with a dimensional squeegee carried by the pointer. Each sweep reveals a Higgsfield-generated Sonoran daydream. Treat this as a brand illustration, never proof of a customer result.
- Native scrolling drives image scale, a gently rotating crew stamp, staggered service photos and horizontal type. A short sticky chapter gives the cleaning scene room to breathe. Keep controls at the edges and preserve ordinary scrolling. Honor reduced motion and save-data preferences.

## Reusable assets

`public/brand/logo-long.svg`, `logo-long-outline.svg`, `logo-stacked.svg`, `logo-stacked-outline.svg`, `wildcat-mascot.png` and `wildcat-club.png` are retained from supplied project assets. `src/styles/brand.css` carries the shared web system; `src/styles/immersive.css` adds the immersive composition. The generated scene and provenance are documented in `ASSET-PROVENANCE.md`.

## Expansion

Keep this visual language consistent across every new service location. Adapt the local photos and factual service-area copy; keep the same logo, blue, type, crew voice, guarantee language and quote flow.

## The wash world

Use a shared water motif across the homepage: slowly descending translucent soap sheets, dimensional water beads and thin trails. Keep them behind the content, pause them offscreen, and provide a page-motion pause control. Reduced-motion visitors receive static surfaces.

Photographs sit in paper mats with ink edges and cyan or navy offset shadows. Native scrolling passes a rinse highlight across the image; pointer movement adds a small tilt and localized reflection. Actual job images always remain intact.

Reviews fill the page width (swipeable on phones). Pair the service-area directory with the supplied crew/truck photograph, using two short columns of locations. Compact FAQ panels replace the repeated heading-and-tall-list layout. The cyan wordmark strip stays continuously animated.
